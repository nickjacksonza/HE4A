## Key Takeaways

- Executive coaching is a structured, confidential partnership. It isn't consulting, and it isn't therapy.
- Most engagements run three to twelve months, with about one session a month.
- Coaching helps most with isolation, imposter syndrome and the pressure of leading through funding cuts.
- It works best for leaders who are ready for the next level and have a clear goal.

The executive director of an HIV organization has a board to answer to, a funder to satisfy, staff to keep and a community that depends on them. Who do they call when the hardest decision of the month is about all of those at once?

That question is why more HIV nonprofit leaders are asking about executive coaching. Here's what it is, how it works, and what to expect.

## What Executive Coaching Is

Coaching is a structured process that helps a leader build skills, change how they think about a problem, and reach goals they've agreed on with the coach. It differs from consulting, where an expert hands you answers, and from therapy, which looks at your past. A coach helps you find your own answer and then keeps you accountable for acting on it.

Coaches offer different things. One nonprofit coach describes the benefits as working through limiting beliefs like imposter syndrome, ending the isolation that executive directors often feel, and having a trusted partner for growth. Others focus on helping leaders manage stress and conflict, or lead without burning out.

## Why HIV Leaders Are Asking Now

The HIV response is under pressure. In a January 2026 article for TheBody, Masen Davis of Funders Concerned About AIDS said that HIV organizations across the U.S., particularly in the South, were scaling back services, furloughing staff, reducing testing and outreach, and canceling prevention programs as public funding was delayed, cut or made uncertain. The same article describes many executive leaders leaving their roles over the past year, with successors stepping into a hard environment.

New leaders, first-time executive directors and leaders who have held the role for years all face the same thing: decisions with no easy answer, made in public, with few people they can speak to openly. A coach is one of those people.

## How an Engagement Usually Works

Firms describe similar shapes:

- **A first conversation.** You and the coach check that you're a good fit. Some firms offer this as an introductory session.
- **A coaching agreement.** It sets out how you'll work together and how your conversations stay confidential.
- **Discovery.** A short interview, or a longer survey of the people you work with.
- **A plan.** Smaller steps toward the larger goals you've named.
- **Regular sessions.** Typically about an hour, once a month, in person, by video or by phone. A session often opens with a check-in, works on a challenge you bring, and closes with next steps.
- **A fixed term.** Three or six months is common. Longer engagements run to twelve months.

Our own approach follows this shape. We offer confidential monthly sessions for executive directors and senior staff, especially people who are the first to hold their role in the organization's history. Most of our engagements run three to nine months.

## What Coaching Can and Can't Do

It can give you a thinking partner, a plan and accountability. It can help you prepare for a hard board conversation, a layoff decision or a transition.

It can't fix a broken board, replace a missing budget or do the work for you. If your board and your executive director disagree on who decides what, coaching may help you, but a [board governance review](/non-profit-leadership.html#board-governance-review) will fix the structure. Many leaders do both.

Coaching also suits leaders who are ready to grow. One guide for nonprofit leaders says plainly that it's meant for high performers ready for the next level, not for fixing someone who is struggling in the job.

## What It Costs

Prices vary a lot, and most coaches price a package rather than an hour. One nonprofit coach, writing from his own experience, gives a range of roughly $2,500 to $5,000 for a six-month engagement at the low end, with twelve-month engagements costing about double. Treat that as one person's observation, not a standard rate. Some funders and foundations offer coaching to the leaders they support, so ask yours.

## Is It for You?

Ask yourself:

1. Is there a decision, a transition or a pattern I keep circling?
2. Do I have someone I can speak to without worrying about how it sounds?
3. Am I ready to try something different and be held to it?

If you answered yes to the first two and no to the third, say so in your first conversation. A good coach will start there. If you'd like to talk about [executive coaching for HIV nonprofit leaders](/non-profit-leadership.html#executive-coaching), [start a conversation with us](/contact.html).
